Electroencephalography (EEG) is currently the most widely used non-invasive method for detecting cognitive activity. Being an inherently high-dimensional data generator, a sufficiently robust feature extractor is needed to capture as much information as possible. Topological Data Analysis (TDA) serves as a tool for capturing higher-order relationships between points, as well as their joint geometry. In this paper, we ask whether the functional connectivity of EEG recordings carries sufficient information to discriminate cognitive states. The aim of this research lies in topology’s contribution to the representation of the data, not in the classifier itself. Using a conventional SVM, the TDA pipeline classified EEG recordings from 36 subjects as either rest or mental arithmetic with an accuracy of 0.89 [0.79-0.97], matching a band-power baseline tuned identically (0.90). This result shows that the geometry of coordinated cortical activity carries sufficient information to discriminate distinct cognitive states, matching representations that use electrode identity.
